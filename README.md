## What Explains Variation in Conflict-Related Sexual Violence Across Countries?

This project investigates whether pre-conflict sexual violence prevalence helps explain variation in conflict-related sexual violence (CRSV) across countries. Drawing on the continuum of violence framework, the study hypothesizes that societies with higher levels of sexual violence before conflict may also experience higher levels of sexual violence during conflict.

Using data from Butler and Jones (2016) and the Global Burden of Disease dataset, the analysis examines the relationship between pre-conflict sexual violence exposure and the severity of CRSV perpetrated by government security forces against civilians. The dependent variable is measured on a five-point ordinal scale (0–4), while the independent variable is the log-transformed, age-standardized Summary Exposure Value (SEV) for sexual violence in the year before conflict.

The project combines cross-national data and quantitative statistical analysis to explore whether pre-existing patterns of sexual violence are associated with wartime violence, contributing to research on the factors that shape CRSV across conflicts.

Methods and tools: R, data cleaning and merging, descriptive statistics, and bivariate ANOVA.
